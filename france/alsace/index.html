    Alsace.

    The Alsace vineyards run for 120 kilometres along the foot of the Vosges, from Marlenheim near Strasbourg to Thann in the south, with an outpost around Wissembourg in the far north: 15,500 hectares of vines in 119 villages of the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin. Alsace is white-wine country, and its wines are mostly named after the grape – Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris, Muscat, Pinot Blanc and Sylvaner, with Pinot Noir for the reds. There are three main appellations: Alsace (71% of the production), Crémant d’Alsace (25%) and Alsace Grand Cru (4%), from 51 named vineyards on the best slopes. The Route des Vins d’Alsace, opened in 1953, links the villages for 170 kilometres, past some 1,000 producers – family estates, merchant houses and cooperatives – most of whom welcome visitors in their cellars. 124 producers from Falstaff’s guide and OpenStreetMap on one map, with restaurants, places to stay and directions by train, bus and bike.
